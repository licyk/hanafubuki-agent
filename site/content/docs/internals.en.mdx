## Staged commits and the drop guard [#staged-commits]

Every mutation of a session is durable before it is visible ([Sessions and turns](/docs/architecture#sessions-and-turns)). It works like this (`service/durable.rs`, `service/state/cell.rs` in the kernel):

- A session record keeps its identity apart from five groups: settings, status, conversation, event journal and runtime (`service/state/record.rs`). A rollback restores the groups whole.
- **Staging.** A mutation changes the record under its cell's lock and is staged as a `StagedPersistence`: the checkpoint (`AgentConversationCommit`) or delta (`AgentConversationDelta`) to append, and the state to roll back to. While a commit is staged, the record's `persisting_revision` holds every other writer back: they lock the record only once nothing is staged (`lock_after_persistence`).
- **Settling.** The append's acknowledgement (`AgentHistoryAppendAck`) settles the commit: on success the persisted revision is published (`persisting_revision` cleared); on failure the record is rolled back, if its revision is still the staged one. Only then may the caller emit the commit's events. An acknowledgement the store drops without answering rolls the record back and fails as `ShuttingDown`.
- **Async callers** (the turn loop, its admission in the kernel's `start_turn`, compaction, execution progress) queue the append (`AgentHistoryStore::enqueue_commit` or `enqueue_delta`) and await the settlement's answer. The settlement runs inside the acknowledgement, on the history writer that made the append, not in the awaiting future: it happens even if the future is dropped meanwhile, and nothing waiting on the session depends on the turn's runtime making progress.
- **Synchronous callers** append on the calling thread (`append_commit` or `append_delta`) and settle there: the service's session mutations, which a host runs off the async runtime, and the turn submission (`submit_turn_with_sink`), which the facade runs on its blocking boundary, so a turn's admission and the spawn of its managed task are never separated.

**Drop guards.** A turn or a compaction dropped before its end (its task aborted, or a `start_turn` dropped while its admission is being appended) ends through its guard (`TurnExecutionGuard`, `CompactionExecutionGuard`), which can neither await nor block. A turn's guard first aborts the turn's pending approvals. The guard then stages the cancellation and hands it to the store with nothing awaiting it (`StagedPersistence::into_follow_up`); the acknowledgement publishes it and then makes it visible itself, on the writer's thread: it wakes the terminal waiters, emits the events and notifies the observers (`TurnFinished`).

If a commit was staged when the guard dropped, the guard cannot wait for it, so it defers its commit on the cell (`SessionCell::stage_or_defer`). Whatever settles the staged commit (publishing it or rolling it back) stages the first deferred commit that still applies, under the same lock, and the acknowledgement hands it back to the store as an `AgentHistoryFollowUp`. The writer appends a follow-up next, before it takes its next command, and acknowledges it in turn, so a chain of follow-ups is appended in order. A store that stops appending fails the follow-up with the commit it follows.

## Execution progress [#execution-progress]

A tool reports progress (a process's start, output, status, disposal) through `AgentToolProgressSink::report`, which stays synchronous: it is called from the tool's call on the turn's async worker, or from a monitor thread that outlives the call. So `report` only queues the update on the session's cell (`service/progress.rs`):

- **One consumer per session.** A report that finds no consumer running spawns one on the runtime of the turn that created the sink; it applies and commits the queued updates in order, awaiting each append, and ends when the queue is empty. A consumer that stopped with the queue not empty (aborted, or its runtime shut down) lets the update it had taken settle on its own; the next report, or a flush on its own runtime, starts a new one.
- **The bound.** The queue holds 64 updates (`EXECUTION_PROGRESS_QUEUE_CAPACITY`). Past that, an output update is coalesced into the queued output of the same execution when nothing of that execution is queued after it: its text is appended, keeping the newest 16 KiB (`MAXIMUM_COALESCED_OUTPUT_BYTES`). Every other update (an execution's start, status, disposal, a cleanup failure) is queued even then, so the queue holds at most its capacity, those updates, and one output after each.
- **Order with the turn.** A turn awaits every update queued before its terminal commit (`flush_execution_progress`), so what its calls reported is committed first. A dropped turn cannot await, so the updates still queued then are committed after its cancellation, still in order.
- **The application's own reports** (a background terminal's stop, start-up recovery) go through `report_session_execution_progress`, which waits up to 5 s for the updates queued before it and commits on the calling thread.
- **Closing.** A session's removal and `shutdown_history` close its queue and abort its consumer; after that `report` returns `false`. `stop_operations` first flushes what was queued until then, within the shutdown deadline.

## Request images [#request-images]

The image attachments of a model request are read with no session lock held (`service/sampling/images.rs`). A sampling step, an automatic compaction's estimate and a compaction:

<Steps>
  <Step>
    collect, under the session's lock, the attachments the history references (each once, in order);
  </Step>
  <Step>
    release the lock while the history store loads them
    (`AgentHistoryStore::enqueue_image_attachment_load`): the file store reads them, with their
    digest and image checks, on the session's writer and acknowledges their bytes in order, so the
    kernel reads no attachment file itself; the trait's default, for a store held in memory, loads
    them inline;
  </Step>
  <Step>
    lock again, check that what they were building for is unchanged, and assemble the request from
    the loaded bytes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

An attachment the history references but that was not loaded means the history changed in between, and the request fails with `StaleRevision`. Attachments are content-addressed, so the request is byte for byte the one a read under the lock would have built. A load that failed fails the request only if the request uses that attachment. A compaction keeps its loaded images for its remote replacement (Responses compaction) and for its estimate of the next request.

## Waiting on a session [#session-waits]

A session cell has two signals: `persisted`, which advances when the staged commit settles, and `terminal`, which advances when a turn or a compaction reaches a terminal state. Both advance when the session is removed, and a removed cell answers `SessionNotFound` to whoever still holds it. Each signal has an async side (a `watch` epoch) and a blocking side (a `Condvar` on the cell's lock); `wait_until` waits on `persisted` while a commit is staged and on `terminal` otherwise.

- **Async waits:** the turn loop, its admission and compaction (`lock_after_persistence`), cancellation's confirmation (`wait_until` under a Tokio timeout), and a required compaction waiting for the background one (`wait_until`, which the turn's cancellation wakes by advancing `terminal`).
- **Blocking waits** are for callers off the async runtime only: the synchronous service methods a host runs on blocking workers, and the facade's blocking boundary, which also runs a session's activation before a model operation and submits turns; `list_snapshots`; `flush_for_diagnostics`; and `stop_operations` and `shutdown` on the shutdown thread, where every session's wait, its progress flush and then the waits for the managed turn tasks and the background compaction tasks (each their own `Condvar`) share one deadline.
- **Never blocking:** the drop guards, execution progress and the kernel's async `start_turn` wait on no blocking side.

`cancel_turn_confirmed` requests the cancellation, waits 500 ms for the turn to end cooperatively (`COOPERATIVE_GRACE`), then aborts its managed task (unless the turn has already staged its end, under the record's lock: it emits that end's events once the commit is durable, which a slow disk can hold past the grace, and an abort would drop them) and waits for the rest of a 5 s deadline (`CONFIRMATION_DEADLINE`) for the cancellation to be durable; past it, `CancellationNotConfirmed`. Both waits are async, so a cancel holds no blocking worker.

## The history store's refusals [#history-refusals]

`FileAgentHistoryStore` (`hanafubuki-agent-history`): one JSONL rollout per session is canonical, and the SQLite index (`index.sqlite3`) a projection of the rollouts ([Storage](/docs/architecture#storage)). What the store cannot use costs only its session, never the store and never a file. Only the index or the layout (its schema, SQL, directory I/O) fails the store's open, and with it the Agent's construction.

### What open cannot admit [#history-open]

| Found at open                                                                                                                              | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| an index it cannot use                                                                                                                     | discarded and rebuilt from the rollouts, every session judged again; `open_rebuilding_index` (a restart with `rebuild_history_index`) discards it on request                                                                                                           |
| a rollout of another format (`IncompatibleFormat`)                                                                                         | moved, with the session's attachment directory, into a `.recovery` directory beside it (`quarantine_file`: `<name>.<pid>.<n>.invalid`, which no scan reads); the new path is logged and is part of the session's reason; a move that fails leaves the file where it is |
| a rollout that does not project (undecodable, or failing the record checks of session, ordinal and checkpoint identity, which stay strict) | left in place with a pending repair (the `agent_projection_repairs` table), which every later start retries                                                                                                                                                            |
| a file that is missing, a session with copies in both the active and the archived tree, a path outside the store                           | left as it is; a rollout whose lifecycle and tree merely disagree (an archive that stopped part-way) is moved to the right tree instead                                                                                                                                |
| a committed item, or an image attachment it references, that does not read                                                                 | left as it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

Each such session keeps its id reserved (`next_session_id` is raised past it, so a new session never shares its id, rollout name or attachments), is refused by transcript and runtime reads (`ProjectionUnavailable` with its reason), and is returned to recovery as a failure, which quarantines it; the other sessions open, and so does the Agent. Nothing of it is rewritten or deleted.

### Refused sessions [#history-refused]

A session's projection is unavailable for one of two reasons (`ProjectionLag`), and each is cleared by something different:

- **An append's lag.** An append whose record is durable but whose projection failed refuses the session's reads only until its writer projects the rollout again: on a later append, or when the writer stops.
- **A refusal.** A session the index could not admit (at open, or later by the session list) stays refused until a repair (`repair_session_projection`) or the next start judges it again. A refusal is never the writer's to clear.

The session list leaves out a row whose summary does not decode and refuses that session the same way. It is logged once and told to the store's `AgentHistoryRefusalSink` (`open_reporting_refusals`), through which the slot adds it, with its reason, to the Agent status's quarantined sessions; the row keeps its place in the paging, so the cursor still passes it and the rest of the list returns. A rebuilt index projects the summary again.

### The attachment sweep [#attachment-sweep]

The sweep of unreferenced attachment blobs (at open, and for a session whose turn admission failed) only collects garbage: it deletes a blob only when no session file still on disk references it.

- A blob lives under the session that staged it, but any session may reference it (a fork child keeps the source session of a restored prompt's images). So the sweep keeps what any projected item references, and every 64-digit hexadecimal run that a rollout moved into `.recovery` contains, whichever session owns the blob. Those files are read once per open, as bytes, never decoded; one that cannot be read keeps every blob. Such a blob is freed once that file is restored or removed.
- It deletes nothing while any session's projection is unavailable (that session's references are unknown), nothing of a session the index does not hold, and skips, with a warning, a session it cannot sweep. An unexpected file among a session's blobs is kept.

### Deleting a session [#history-deletion]

A deletion (`delete_sessions_confirmed`) is refused while a session outside it still needs one of its sessions:

- `ReferencedByFork`: a fork in the lineage table (`agent_session_lineage`) that is not being deleted with it;
- `ReferencedByQuarantinedSession`: a session file the index does not hold (a rollout moved into `.recovery`, or one refused at open and left in place with a pending repair) that forks from it (a `forkedFromSessionId` or `historyBase` `sessionId`) or names one of its blobs' digests. The error names each such file and its session. These files are read as bytes, not decoded (`store/kept_files.rs`), and a file that cannot be read refuses the deletion, since what it needs is unknown.

Nothing of either session is touched. Restored and opened, such a fork is in the lineage table again; removed, it keeps nothing. The kernel's `prepare_session_deletion` asks the store first (`check_sessions_deletable`), so a refused deletion stops before the application revokes the session's memory or stops its terminals, and its reason is the delete command's error.

A session's persisted descendants are deleted with it, in the same `delete_sessions_confirmed`: the facade lists them through the index's `parent_session_id` (both lifecycles, recursively), loads each, and passes them to `prepare_session_deletion` as the session's `descendants`, which checks the whole set and marks each disposing. A child forked from its parent therefore never refuses the parent's deletion, while a fork outside the set still does. The facade then stops each member's terminals and revokes its memory, and any failure cancels the whole deletion. `complete_session_deletion_confirmed` checks that every member still stands as prepared, with no turn running, before it deletes anything; one that does not cancels the deletion with nothing deleted. Archiving is a cascade of ordinary archives instead, children before their parent, skipping those already archived. Both first close the live ephemeral children of every member.

A deletion that goes ahead first writes an obligation (`trash/delete-<millis>-<n>/obligation.json`), moves the rollouts and each session's whole attachment directory into that directory, removes the sessions' index rows in one transaction, deletes the moved files, and removes the obligation last. An obligation left by a deletion that stopped part-way is completed at the next open; one that does not decode is quarantined.

### Writers [#history-writers]

Writes go through one writer thread per session (`agent-history-writer-<id>`, a queue of 64 commands), which acknowledges each append once it and its projection are written.

- `append_commit` and `append_delta` wait for the acknowledgement. `enqueue_commit` and `enqueue_delta` only find or start the writer (reading the index for the session's file when none runs) and queue, holding the session's gate no longer than that; a writer's queue that is full makes the caller wait for room.
- A writer whose thread ended (a panic, logged and reported as a kernel fault by its `BackgroundWorkerGroup`) is withdrawn and replaced by the next command that holds the session's gate, so one failed write does not stop the session's history.
- `shutdown(deadline)` queues each writer's stop behind its commands and waits until the deadline; a writer still busy then is reported (`WriterDidNotStop`) and left detached. Nothing durable is lost: a commit is acknowledged only once it is on disk, so only appends a writer had not acknowledged (never durable for any turn; recovery restores the last acknowledged revision) remain unwritten.

## MCP settings repairs and diagnostics [#mcp-settings-diagnostics]

### Repairs [#mcp-settings-repairs]

`mcp.toml` ([its keys](/docs/config-reference#mcp-toml)) is the Agent's own data, so `McpSettingsStore` (`hanafubuki-agent-config`) reads it tolerantly through `hanafubuki_agent_mcp::repair_settings`, entry by entry:

| Found                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Repair (`McpSettingsRepairKind`)                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| a file that is not TOML, not UTF-8 or over 8 MiB                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | the defaults replace it (`reset`)                                                              |
| one of the five timeouts outside 1–86400, `maximumConcurrentCalls` or a `toolMaximumConcurrentCalls` value outside 1–64, a credential's `generation` below 1 or not a whole number                                                                                                                             | moved to the nearest bound; a fractional value inside the range is truncated (`value_clamped`) |
| a server past the 64th, one that does not decode, one that fails `McpServerConfig::validate` (a URL with credentials or a fragment, overlapping public and credential headers or variables, the stdio program, argument and environment rules, the filter and policy bounds), a second server with the same id | that server is dropped alone (`server_dropped`)                                                |
| a credential with an invalid key, one that does not decode, an unusable value                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | that credential is dropped alone (`credential_dropped`)                                        |

A value that is not a number where a number belongs is left for the decoder, which drops the entry. Unknown keys are logged and stay in the file. Before the repaired values are written, the original is kept in `.recovery/` ([Configuration recovery](/docs/recovery#backup)). Writes stay strict: a server the user adds or edits is validated, never clamped.

Each repair is an `McpSettingsRepair`: its kind, its subject (the server id or credential key, when the entry has a valid one), the field a clamp changed, and why, never a value from the file. The next read finds a clean file, so the store keeps the repairs (up to 256, `MAX_RETAINED_REPAIRS`) for as long as the Agent that opened it runs. They reach a host as `McpApplicationSnapshot.settingsRepairs` (Hanafubuki's MCP settings panel shows them until dismissed), and the standalone binary's `doctor` lists them among the configuration's recoveries. The snapshot's `serverDefaults` (`McpServerDefaults::default()`: enabled, optional, the legacy protocol, timeouts 15/30/30/120/10 s, 4 concurrent calls, `ask`, `deferred`, OAuth `disabled`) is what a host's form for a new server starts from, so no frontend owns a default.

### Diagnostics [#mcp-diagnostics]

`McpDiagnosticStore` keeps a ring of the latest 128 diagnostics per server (`MCP_DIAGNOSTIC_CAPACITY`): each with its stage (`spawn`, `initialize`, `discover_tools`, `tool_call`, `connection_lost` and the others), outcome (`success`, `warning`, `error`, `cancelled`, `outcome_unknown`), code, message, transport, the configuration and connection generations, the tool, whether it is retryable and the next step. A message is cut to 2 KiB and sanitized: the word after `Bearer`, a word that starts with `token=`, `password:` or the like (`access_token`, `refresh_token`, `secret`, `authorization`, `code`, `verifier`), and a URL's query are replaced by `[redacted]`. A stdio server's standard error is kept as a tail of its last 128 lines, at most 16 KiB, sanitized the same way.

A server's status line is its `McpDiagnosticSummary`: its current diagnostics, those recorded since its latest connection attempt began (`begin_attempt`; before the first, all of them), counted as errors (`error`, `outcome_unknown`) and warnings, with a headline that is the most severe of them, the most recent of that severity. It carries every current entry, so a host can open the line to all of them; the whole ring stays in the server's diagnostic snapshot. It reaches a host in `McpApplicationSnapshot.diagnostics`, one per configured server in settings order. The terminal interface's `/mcp` shows each server's phase and last error, not the summary.

## Skill file issues [#skill-file-issues]

`SKILL.md` frontmatter and `agents/openai.yaml` are written by people and other tools, so `hanafubuki-agent-skills` reads them one known key at a time (`yaml_fields.rs`): a value of the wrong type costs only that key, and a `null` reads as absent. What the user wrote and [the fields](/docs/skills#skill-md) are on the Skills page; the rules per field:

| In                                                                      | Found                                                                                                                                                 | Result                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `SKILL.md`                                                              | not UTF-8, no `---` frontmatter, frontmatter that is not a mapping                                                                                    | the Skill fails to load                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `SKILL.md`                                                              | frontmatter that is not valid YAML                                                                                                                    | read again with the scalars that read as prose but parse as YAML structure quoted (a value with `: ` in it, one that starts with `[`, `{`, `@`, `*`, `&` or a backtick and does not parse); still invalid, the Skill fails |
| `name`, `description`                                                   | not a string; no description                                                                                                                          | the Skill fails: these make it selectable                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `name`                                                                  | over 96 characters                                                                                                                                    | cut, with an issue                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `description`                                                           | over 4096 bytes                                                                                                                                       | cut on a character boundary, with an issue                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `license`, `metadata`, `metadata.version`, `metadata.short-description` | the wrong type                                                                                                                                        | that key ignored, with an issue (`ignored metadata.version: expected a string, found a number`)                                                                                                                            |
| `license`, `metadata.version`                                           | over 256 or 128 bytes                                                                                                                                 | dropped, with no issue                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `openai.yaml`                                                           | not UTF-8, not YAML, not a mapping                                                                                                                    | the whole file ignored, with one issue; the Skill loads from `SKILL.md`                                                                                                                                                    |
| `openai.yaml`                                                           | over 64 KiB, unreadable                                                                                                                               | ignored, with no issue                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `openai.yaml`                                                           | `interface.*`, `policy.allow_implicit_invocation`, `dependencies.tools` or one of its entries of the wrong type                                       | that field ignored, with an issue                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `openai.yaml`                                                           | a display name over 256 bytes, other text over 4096, an icon path that is absolute or escapes the Skill, a brand colour that is not `#rrggbb`         | dropped, with no issue                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `openai.yaml`                                                           | a dependency whose type is not `mcp` or whose server id is invalid, one past the 32nd; a dependency's URL that is not `http(s)`, an unknown transport | the dependency, or that field, dropped with no issue                                                                                                                                                                       |
| a Skill root                                                            | a directory below it that cannot be read                                                                                                              | skipped, with an issue; an unreadable root fails that root                                                                                                                                                                 |

An issue is a `SkillLoadIssue` in `SkillCatalogSnapshot::issues`, with the path of the file or directory and a `severity`: `warning` for what loaded anyway (a field ignored or cut, a Skill shadowed by a nearer one with the same id, the watcher's diagnostic), `error` for a directory that was not loaded (a Skill that failed, a directory or root that could not be read). An unreadable directory below a root is therefore an `error` issue, though it costs only itself. The scan goes 8 directories deep and reads a `SKILL.md` of at most 1 MiB. `allowed-tools` is not parsed.

## Tolerant decoding of provider streams [#provider-decoding]

A Provider's stream is external input ([The model API](/docs/architecture#the-model-api)): a value the Agent cannot use does not fail the response it came in when the rest is usable (`tolerant_decode.rs` and each protocol's decoder in `hanafubuki-agent-model-api`). The identities (response, item and call ids) stay strict.

| every protocol     | empty or whitespace-only tool arguments                                                                                                                                           | `{}`, a call without arguments                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| every protocol     | tool arguments that are not JSON (on Responses also: not a string)                                                                                                                | `{}` plus an `argument_error` with the parser's message and a preview; the call still pairs with an output that tells the model                                                                                                                                                                   |
| every protocol     | a usage with one count missing or `null`                                                                                                                                          | that count 0, the other kept                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| every protocol     | a usage that is not an object, a count that is not a whole number, a usage with neither count                                                                                     | no usage                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Chat Completions   | a chunk without `choices` (`stream_options.include_usage`)                                                                                                                        | its usage recorded; only choice 0 is read                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Chat Completions   | a tool delta without `index`                                                                                                                                                      | it continues the latest call, unless it carries a call id other than that call's, which starts the next one; ids that arrive in fragments are joined, and a whole id sent again is kept once                                                                                                      |

| Anthropic Messages | a `thinking` block's `signature` (its start's, then each `signature_delta`) and a `redacted_thinking` block's `data`                                                              | kept whole on the reasoning item (`ModelReasoningReplay`, never serialized) to be sent back; a block without a signature or data, or over 1 MiB, is shown but not sent back                                                                                                                       |

In the model listing a flag is a boolean or Anthropic's `{"supported": boolean}`, and an empty list, a list with no string, or another value says nothing. A yes wins over a no because a wrong yes costs one refused request, while a wrong no would keep images or reasoning from a model that takes them. The listing fails only on a page without a `data` array or over its bounds (2 MiB a page, 1000 entries, 10 Anthropic pages); an Anthropic page with `has_more` but no `last_id` ends it with what was read. Thinking's events per protocol are in [Thinking](/docs/architecture#thinking).

## Model metadata [#model-metadata]

The registry ([Model metadata](/docs/architecture#model-metadata)) is `kernel/src/model_metadata/`; the facade's store is `model_metadata_store.rs`.

- **A listing merges model by model.** A model the listing names takes that entry's facts, those it no longer reports included (they become unknown); a model it leaves out keeps what earlier listings reported for 7 days after it was last listed, while a model the latest listing names keeps it until a listing no longer does. A listing the registry records always replaces the endpoint's (protocol and base URL) list of models.
- **The shrink guard** (after Hoshimi): a listing with fewer than half the models of the endpoint's last recorded one (more likely a listing cut short than a Provider dropping half its models) is not recorded, once; the next listing is, whatever it says, so a Provider that really dropped its models is believed the second time. Only the count matters: a listing of as many other models (a local server restarted with another model) is recorded at once. `list_provider_models` returns the listing either way. A draft's listing (`test_provider_draft`) is recorded like any other.
- **Expiry and bounds.** A learned fact holds 7 days from when it was learned, unless it is forgotten first (`forget_learned`, for one route or every route of an endpoint: `providers forget`, `/model forget`, the facade's `forget_learned_model_facts`), which drops the routes' learned facts, keeps their reported ones and saves as any change does (nothing to forget, no save). The registry keeps 4096 routes and 32 endpoints' listings at most, dropping the ones observed or listed longest ago, never the route it just learned about. Expired facts are dropped at the next change and ignored before it.
- **models.dev** (`model-api/src/models_dev.rs`) is external data, read tolerantly: `api.json` is read one provider at a time; a provider that is not an object, a model that is not one, and a field not of its type (`limit.context`, `limit.output`, `reasoning`, `modalities.input`) are left out or unknown, a window outside 1024 to 20,000,000 tokens or an output of 0 is unknown, and the count left out is logged at debug. It fails only when the body is not a JSON object or names no usable model, over 32 MiB, after a minute, or on a status other than 2xx; the copy kept is then used. What is kept per provider is the host of its `api` and, per normalized id, the window, the largest output and a "yes" to reasoning and to images (a "no" is not kept); two ids of one provider that normalize alike are merged as two providers' are (the smaller limit, any "yes"). The copy lives a week from its download (a download time ahead of the clock counts from now) and is dropped at the next change after; `forget_learned` never touches it.
- **The file** (`model-metadata.json`) is `{"version": 1, "routes": [...], "listings": [...], "modelsDev": {...}}`: each route its key (the endpoint's protocol and `baseUrlSha256`, and the model id) and its `reported` and `learned` facts, each fact its value, source and `observedAtMs`; each listing its endpoint, `listedAtMs`, `models` and whether a shrunk listing is being `held`; the copy of models.dev (when there is one) its `fetchedAtMs` and `catalog`, which a copy that does not read leaves out (it is downloaded again). It is read once, when the Agent is built: an unknown field is ignored, a route or listing that does not read is left out and a fact that does not read is unknown, a fact's source is taken from its group and its time is never later than now. A file over 16 MiB, not a JSON object, or not of version 1 is quarantined into `.recovery/` and the run starts with nothing known.
- **Writes** leave every caller at once: the registry hands its store a snapshot after each change (under its lock, so in order), and the store passes it to a writer in the Agent's worker group, which writes the newest one waiting (a temporary file renamed over the old one) and, when the Agent stops, the last. A failed write is logged at warn and changes nothing else. A run that records nothing writes nothing.

## A refusal as too long [#context-refusals]

Whether a Provider's error refuses a request as too long for the model's context window is decided in `hanafubuki-agent-model-api` (`context_overflow.rs`, after Hoshimi's `core/failure.py`), for every protocol's error, an HTTP refusal or an error in the stream (`classify_remote_model_error`, which asks it before the image refusal). It reads, in order:

| Step                                 | Rule                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| a 429                                | never, whatever the code or the message                                                                                                                            |
| the code                             | `context_length_exceeded`, `context_window_exceeded`, `prompt_too_long` (any case): yes, whatever the status                                                       |
| the status                           | anything but 400, 413 and 422 is no; an error in the stream has none and is read                                                                                   |
| a rate limit or quota                | `rate limit`, `rate_limit`, `ratelimit`, `quota`, `tokens per min`, `requests per min`, `too many requests`, `限流`, `限速`, `额度` in the message or the code: no |
| the code and the message, lowercased | yes when either holds one of the wordings below (`token_limit_exceeded` is OpenRouter's code)                                                                      |

The wordings: `context_length_exceeded`, `context_window_exceeded`, `context window exceeded`, `maximum context`, `input exceeds the context window`, `exceeds the context length`, `exceed`, `exceeds` or `exceeded` followed by `context limit` or `the context limit`, `prompt is too long`, `input is too long`, `too many tokens`, `too many input tokens`, `token limit exceeded`, `token_limit_exceeded`, `input token count`, `exceeds the maximum number of tokens`, `maximum input length`, `maximum prompt length`, `上下文长度`, `上下文窗口`, `超出最大长度`, `超过最大长度`, `超过了最大`, `输入过长`, `超出` with `长度限制` at most six characters after it, and `token` followed by `数` or `数量` and `超过` or `超出` (spaces allowed after `token`). Hoshimi's bare `context length` and `context window` are left out, since a refusal about something else may name the window (`context length must be a positive integer`), and so is `request too large`, which is also how OpenAI words a rate limit per minute and how a size limit in bytes is worded. Such an error stays `ModelError::Remote`, as before.

`context_limit_in(message)` reads the window a refusal names. Its patterns are tried in turn, each at its first match, and a value outside 1024..=20,000,000 (`PLAUSIBLE_CONTEXT_WINDOW`) moves on to the next pattern; a number is a digit, then digits and commas, the commas ignored:

| Pattern                                                                                                                               | Example                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| `maximum context length is N` (or `prompt` or `input length`)                                                                         | `This model's maximum context length is 131072 tokens`   |
| `context window`, `length` or `limit`, then `of` or `is`, then N                                                                      | `exceeds the context window of 200,000 tokens`           |
| `> N maximum`                                                                                                                         | `prompt is too long: 210000 tokens > 200000 maximum`     |
| `maximum number of tokens allowed`, `maximum allowed tokens`, `maximum input token(s)`, then `is`, `(` or `:` if there is one, then N | `exceeds the maximum number of tokens allowed (1048576)` |
| `limit` or `最大`, at most twelve characters and no digit, then N of four characters or more, then `tok`                              | `输入超过最大长度限制 32768 tokens`                      |

The kernel learns from it ([Learned windows](/docs/architecture#model-metadata), `service/context_window.rs`): the named window, else the refused request's estimate within the same bounds (a request under 1024 tokens refused as too long is more likely a misread refusal). The registry keeps a route's learned window only when it is smaller than the live one it has (`learn_context_window`; one past its 7 days no longer counts, and a window no smaller changes and saves nothing). The refused session's commit rewrites its window and its usage's, and its event `model_context_window_learned` ([exec --json events](/docs/json-events#session)) is recorded only when the session took the learned window: a configured session, a window learned larger than the reported one, and a repeat of the window the session has say nothing. Learning logs nothing.

## A refusal of the reasoning parameter [#reasoning-refusals]

Whether a Provider's error refuses the request's reasoning parameter is decided narrowly in `hanafubuki-agent-model-api` (`reasoning_refusal.rs`), after the overflow and the image refusal (`classify_remote_model_error`): a broad match would retry, and teach the route "no reasoning", on an unrelated refusal. It is `ModelError::UnsupportedReasoning` when all of these hold:

| Step              | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| the status        | 400 or 422; an error in the stream, which has none, never counts                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| a parameter named | the lowercased message holds `reasoning`, `thinking`, `effort` (so `reasoning_effort`, `reasoning.effort` and `output_config.effort`) or `output_config`                                                                                                                               |
| a refusal         | the code is `unsupported_parameter` or `unsupported_value` (any case), or the message holds `unsupported`, `not supported`, `does not support`, `doesn't support`, `do not support`, `unknown`, `unrecognized`, `unrecognised`, `not allowed`, `not permitted` or `unexpected keyword` |

So OpenAI's `Unsupported parameter: 'reasoning.effort' is not supported with this model.` (as reported in openai/openai-python#2532) and Anthropic's documented `adaptive thinking is not supported on this model` count, while a refused `temperature`, a budget refused as too small and Anthropic's documented `Invalid signature in thinking block` do not; the suite's other wordings (an unrecognized or unexpected argument, a parameter not allowed) are illustrative, not recorded. A refused level (`unsupported_value`) counts too, as the parameter's refusal. No Chinese wording is matched: none was found in a Provider's refusal.

The kernel (`run_sampling_request`) retries a turn's request once without its reasoning, only when it carried some and the turn is not cancelled, emits `ModelRetryScheduled { stage: ReasoningFallback }`, and the registry learns the route's reasoning as `Unsupported` (`learn_reasoning_unsupported`, a learned "no" that beats a reported "yes", for 7 days). A second refusal, or one of a request without reasoning, fails the turn as `AgentError::UnsupportedReasoning`. A compaction's own request is not retried. A transport that names no route learns nothing, so each of its requests is refused once.

## The crash-loop marker [#crash-loop-marker]

`AgentStartMarker` (`start-marker.json` in the data directory, written with `write_atomic`, pretty-printed) counts the starts that never finished:

```json
{
  "unfinishedStarts": 2,
  "lastAttempt": {
    "pid": 41237,
    "atUnixMillis": 1793260800000
  }
}
```

- A start reads the count, adds one and writes it with this process's `pid` and the time, before it constructs anything. A start refused the data directory's lock records no attempt.
- The attempt is cleared, by writing `{"unfinishedStarts": 0}` (no `lastAttempt`), once the Agent has been `Running` for `AGENT_START_SETTLE` (60 s), when its start failed in an orderly way, when the instance stopped in order (a restart, a fault's stop, a turn off) and at an orderly shutdown. A run that recorded no attempt (the Agent was off) does not clear it, so a crash-loop stop survives orderly exits.
- The last attempt is not counted when the crash record is of the same process and not older than it: a fatal panic is never on an Agent thread (the Agent's are contained), so that run did not die of the Agent. The crash record (`crash-record.json`) is written by the panic hook before a fatal panic aborts, once `configure_crash_record` names it:

```json
{
  "pid": 41237,
  "atUnixMillis": 1793260803512,
  "thread": "main",
  "location": "src/main.rs:42:9",
  "message": "…"
}
```

- At `CRASH_LOOP_STARTS` (3) counted starts, `AgentStartMarker::disabled_reason` leaves the slot `Disabled` with `crashLoop` and logs a notice. The user's restart, or turning the Agent off and on, resets the marker; a mount alone leaves a crash-loop stop as it is. A marker that cannot be read counts no starts: it never keeps the Agent off. A standalone first start runs whatever the count ([The same composition, standalone](/docs/embedding#the-same-composition-standalone)).

## Hierarchical compression [#hierarchical-compression]

The `hierarchical` strategy ([Compaction](/docs/architecture#compaction)) is the kernel's `service/compression/` (the projection in `mod.rs`, the adapter in `adapter.rs`, forks in `inherit.rs`, the content store and the interrupt in `runtime.rs`, the tools in `tools/` and `receipts.rs`, the status in `status.rs`) over the pure `hanafubuki-agent-compression` crate.

**The state.** `PersistedAgentRuntimeState.compression` holds the crate's `State`, re-exported as `AgentCompressionState`: the refs minted over the window, the blocks (deactivated, never deleted), the token snapshots and the nudge's baselines. It is append-mostly: refs are never reissued, blocks are never deleted, and the rest (the nudge, the stats, the latches, the counters) is small. So it is written as a chain of links (`model/compression/chain.rs`), one in each runtime checkpoint that changed it, as `PersistedAgentRuntimeState.compression_update` (`AgentCompressionUpdate`, left out when nothing changed, so a `standard` record has no new field):

- **`snapshot`**: the whole state, its ref map written one way (`by_raw`; `by_ref` is derived when it is read, leaving out `BLOCKED` and the fork's `REF_FLOOR_ID`). It starts a chain.
- **`delta`** (`AgentCompressionDelta`): its place after the snapshot (`seq`, from 1), the ref bindings minted or changed, the blocks made or changed other than in their age (whole), the age of the blocks that only aged (`survived`: every active block ages one step a request), the token counts first rendered and those dropped, and every other field whole when one of them changed.
- **`dropped`**: the state ended (a session that switched away and compacted).

The kernel diffs against the chain's head (`SessionRuntime.compression_head`, `next_link` in `conversation_commit`): the state and `seq` of the last link it staged. A rollback restores the head with the rest of the runtime, so the next link follows the last durable one. There is no head after a load or for a new session (a fork, a child), so their first link is a snapshot; so is a change a delta cannot carry (a binding or a block gone, blocks reordered), and so is the link once the deltas since the snapshot would outweigh it, which keeps reading the chain proportional to the state. A checkpoint that changed nothing carries no link, so a step writes the state once, not with each of its checkpoints. The history store keeps the links in `agent_compression_links` (one row per link, a snapshot or `dropped` replacing the session's rows) and strips the state from `runtime_json`; loading a runtime (`load_runtime_state`, recovery) folds the rows (`AgentCompressionChain`) into `compression`. The in-memory store folds the same way. A chain that does not fold (a delta with no snapshot before it, a `seq` that skips, a delta naming a block or token count the state lacks, a link that cannot be read, `Unreadable`) loads as no state, which the kernel rebuilds (below). A record written before the chain carries the whole state in `compression`, which the store reads as a snapshot. The history format and its version are unchanged; the index's schema is 6. Switching to `hierarchical` installs an empty state when there is none.

**Forks, children and loading.** Item ids survive a fork, so the message ids the state maps do. A fork at the head, and a child at its parent's head (`/side` included), takes its source's state as it is. A fork before a turn (so `edit_from_turn` too) and a child forked before its parent's running turn adopt from it (`inherited_state` over the crate's `adopt_fork_state`, after billion-context's fork adoption): a block whose covered messages are all in the fork is kept, active, with the blocks a tier block consumed as the inactive records they were; an expanded or consumed block, or one covering a message the fork lacks, is dropped. Every message the fork holds keeps its ref and its frozen token count, and the source's highest ref is reserved (`REF_FLOOR_ID` in the ref map), so the fork's own messages number above it. The orphan, truncation, render-deferral and view latches carry over; the nudge's baselines, the stats and the terminal count start over. An empty child has no state. When the source has no state, or a session is loaded without one (none was written, or it could not be read), it is rebuilt by replaying the `compress` calls in the window (`rebuild_state`), with the source's last tuning (the defaults at load) and the compress limits and the recent zone relaxed, since the calls passed them once; a call whose receipt says it failed, was refused or was a no-op is not replayed. A window that holds a compaction (`CompactionSummary`, `ProviderCompaction`) is not rebuilt: after a compaction the refs went on from numbers the window no longer shows, so a replay could fold the wrong messages. A window without `compress` calls gets no state, so a `standard` fork stays without one.

**Which requests.** `CompressionMode::of` decides per request: `Hierarchical` while the strategy is `hierarchical`; `Carry` when a session that switched away still has active blocks, which keeps the projection without the prompt, the nudge or the tools; `Off` otherwise, and whenever the turn has fallen back (`compression_fallback`).

**The adapter.** `MessageLog` maps the window's history items to the crate's messages:

| History item                                                                                                                                        | Message                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `UserMessage`, `SteeringMessage`                                                                                                                    | a user text, its images as attachment handles                                |
| `AssistantMessage`                                                                                                                                  | an assistant text (its `model_text` when it has one)                         |
| `ToolCall`                                                                                                                                          | a tool call: the call id, the name, the arguments as JSON text               |
| `ToolOutput`                                                                                                                                        | a tool result under its call's name: its text parts, structured ones as JSON |
| `CompactionSummary`, `ProviderCompaction`                                                                                                           | a protected user text                                                        |
| `TurnStart`, `WorldStateUpdate`, `SkillContext`, `ContinuationContext`, `PermissionReview`, `ToolSelection`, `CanonicalInitialContext`, `Reasoning` | none: the item keeps its place in the request around the projected ones      |

Every message before the window's first `TurnStart`, a compacted prefix, is protected too: it never folds. A message's id is `<source session id>:<item id>`, unique within the session's lineage. The refs the model reads (`m00001`, …) are not those ids, since item ids are not in history order after a compaction: the crate mints them in history order and never reuses one.

**The projection.** `regular_model_request` builds its input from `projected_history`, which runs the crate's `process_turn_with` over the log, the state, the configuration, the context's size and the session's content store (with no message filters: the Agent has none) (the provider-reported `context_consumed_tokens` of the last response, else an estimate of the window's history). `apply_view` turns the crate's view back into items:

- a covered item is left out;
- a message whose text the view changed gets the new text: the `<acp tokens="…" type="…">mNNNNN</acp>` tag before a user, assistant or tool-output text (an assistant message's `model_text`; a tool output's text parts become one text, its images and resources kept), or a live `compress` call's arguments compacted to the ranges still live. Tool-call arguments are never tagged;
- each summary the view rendered (`[Compressed conversation section] — topic`, for a block with no visible carrier) is inserted at its anchor as a `CompactionSummary` item, which the model API frames as user text.

The stored items never change. `project_model_tool_outputs` then caps the historical tool outputs, as for any request. Under `Hierarchical` the request also gets `build_compress_system_prompt`'s text appended to the instructions, after Plan mode's suffix (built once, the same bytes for every request), and the nudge (its Anthropic wording on Anthropic Messages), or else the status line, as its last input: a user text, never persisted.

**Which request keeps the state.** Building a request advances the state in memory: refs minted for new messages, token snapshots, the nudge's baselines. Only the step's own request, built in `begin_sampling`, keeps it: it is stored on the record and the step's commit persists it. The automatic compaction's estimate and the check after an install build the same request (`step_model_request_projected`) and discard the state, so counting a request never changes it. A step still projects once: the projection is kept on the session (`compression/cache.rs`, in memory only) with what it was made from, the history items, the state and the content store by identity and the settings and token count by value, and the next request built from the same inputs, the step's own after its estimate, takes it instead of projecting again. With message timestamps on, the messages it stamps keep the time of the estimate.

**The tools.** `compress`, `decompress`, `search_context`, `acp_status` and `acp_cache` are `AgentCoreTool`s, planned while the mode is `Hierarchical` (`compression_tools` in the catalog context, the list `planned_tools` makes), in Plan mode too, with `absorb`, `acp_retrieve` and `acp_rule` after them while their features are on (below); all eight names are in `RESERVED_TOOL_NAMES`. Each runs on the session's record under its lock (`execute_compression_tool`), so its hooks get a result. `compress`, `decompress` and the three opt-in tools are then applied again under the lock of the commit that writes their output (`commit_history_with`), against the state of that moment: the output committed is the one of the state committed, bounded (`canonicalize_tool_output`) as a first run's is, and two calls in one step apply in call order. A refused call leaves the state as it was.

- **`compress`** answers with billion-context's receipts: `[Compressed …]` with the summaries' fingerprints, `[Compression FAILED`, `[Compression no-op`.
- **`decompress`** answers in billion-context's host's words, in one of four ways. By default it returns a block's content (one tier up, or every original with `full`) inline as its output and marks an active block restored inline (only the first restore changes the state); a later `compress` over its span refolds it in place under the same id. With `startId` and `endId` (offered in the schema with `imageRef`, the host's extension, `core_tools/compression_spec.rs`) it returns only the block's messages whose refs fall in the range, read from the history (the host needs its content store for this; the Agent keeps the full history), and changes nothing. With a non-blank `toFile` the text goes to the session's export file instead: the value only asks for one, since writing where the model names would be an unapproved write. The tool asks for it (`CompressionToolRun.export`); `execute_compression_tool` writes it with the record's lock released through `AgentHistoryStore::write_session_export`, and the output names the path, or, when the write fails, says so with the first 4 000 characters inline. The file store writes `history/attachments/<session>/exports/decompress-<block>[-full|-<start>-<end>].txt`, replaced by the next export of that name and removed when the session is deleted; a store that keeps no files (the in-memory one, so every ephemeral session) refuses, the default of the trait method. Nothing is spilled to a file unasked, as the host does past 10 000 characters: a request caps its historical tool outputs (`project_model_tool_outputs`), a large inline restore included. With `imageRef` it brings images back: `list` names every message of the window with images (ref, type, size, at most 50), an `mNNNNN` ref or a handle a summary's MEDIA REFS lists (the attachment id the adapter passes as a message's image handle, which the crate appends to a block's summary at a fold) attaches that message's images to the output. A `full` restore and a range attach the images of the messages they return too. The commit charges the turn's tool-output budget (`maximum_tool_output_bytes`) with the output and every image attached to it, and past it fails the turn, so a restore attaches its images in history order only while they fit, with its output, in what is left of the budget when its commit applies it. A note ahead of the body names each image left out by its handle and type, size and dimensions (at most 50), to be restored one at a time by `imageRef`, in a later turn when this one has no room left; an `imageRef` restore none of whose images fits is refused, and the turn goes on. They are the history's own attachments (`CompressionToolRun.images`, written into the committed `ToolOutput`'s `images`), so each later request loads them as any tool output's images and a model without image input gets them stripped; an image restore is refused then.
- **`search_context`** searches the block summaries, active and folded away, then the folded originals (the crate's `folded_message_inputs`, the messages active blocks cover), both by literal occurrences of the query's terms (`Algorithm::Substring`, minimum score 1e-9), as billion-context's host does: one occurrence is a hit and none is no match, where the hybrid search's fuzzy n-grams would make weak matches of a query that matches nothing. A summary line gives the block's covered refs and the passage around its most distinctive term, marked 【】; an original line names a block no summary line listed, its best message's ref and the passage; a query of three or more terms gets the host's tip when a term only lower-ranked blocks hold. The documents' features are cached per session (`SessionRuntime.compression_search`, the crate's `DocCache`, at most 8 Mi UTF-16 units of text), never persisted. The host's plan-aware steering is not ported: it is off unless configured, and the Agent has no such setting.
- **`acp_status`** is the crate's status report. **`acp_cache`** reports from what the Agent records: the last response's usage and the folds. The model API's `ModelUsage` has input and output tokens only, no cached tokens, so none count as cached.

**The opt-in features.** `[compaction.hierarchical]`'s `absorb`, `contentStore`, `crush` and `rules` set the crate's `Config` sections of those names (their other settings keep the crate's defaults: a 4 000-token gate, a 96-character head, 50 rules of 300 characters), and `process_turn_with` applies them in the kernel's order: the content store, the absorbed pairs hidden, crush (only with `absorb`), the absorb demand. They act only under `Hierarchical`: a session that switched away (`Carry`) keeps its absorbed pairs hidden but shows stored originals again and gets no demand, since their tools are no longer planned.

- **`absorb`.** A tool result of 4 000 tokens or more, not protected and not an ACP tool's, gets the `[ACP absorb]` demand appended in the view (never stored), and the instructions get `build_absorb_system_prompt`'s section. The `absorb` call (`parse_absorb_input`, `apply_absorb`) records an `AbsorbRecord` in the state, and from the next request the target's call and result are left out; the `absorb` call stays as the record.
- **`crush`.** Before the demand is decided, a large result's JSON, code or log is folded deterministically (`apply_crush_to_messages`); one that ends under the gate is not asked for.
- **The content store.** A large tool result is replaced in the view by its placeholder when it first appears (`📦 [acp-stored #mNNNNN · kind · tokens tok] …`), and a fold stores the originals its blocks cover (`store_covered_originals`, applied with `compress` at its commit). The store (`AgentContentStore`, the crate's `ContentStore`) is kept in `SessionRuntime.compression_runtime`, not in the records: like the state, only the step's own request keeps the one its projection returns. After a commit that grew it (the step's start, a `compress`) the kernel writes it whole, with the record's lock released, through `AgentHistoryStore::write_compression_store`; the file store writes `history/attachments/<session>/compression-store.json` (staged, published), which the blob sweeps never read and deleting the session removes. A session is loaded with `load_compression_store`; an unreadable file is set aside as `compression-store.unreadable-<millis>.json` and reads as none. A fork or a child takes its source's store. Each entry is kept only while the state binds its ref to the same message (a state rebuilt from the `compress` calls may number differently). The store is a cache of the history: a placeholder is built from the original alone, so an entry lost (a crash before the write, a failed write, an entry dropped) is stored again, byte for byte, at the next request that shows the message, and only a folded original the window no longer holds is lost; `acp_retrieve` then answers that the ref has no stored original. `acp_retrieve` (`apply_retrieve`, `note_retrieval`) returns an original inline, framed as untrusted data, or from 4 000 tokens on writes it to the session's export file `<ref>.txt` (`write_session_export`, as `decompress`'s `toFile`) and answers with `build_retrieval_pointer`'s path; a store that keeps no files returns it inline.
- **`rules`.** `acp_rule` (`execute_rule`) records, deletes, clears or lists `RuleRecord`s in the state. The instructions get `RULES_USAGE_PROMPT` and, once there are rules, `format_rules_for_prompt`'s list, after the static prompt and the absorb section: the same bytes until a rule changes.

**An interrupted turn.** When the user interrupts a `hierarchical` turn, `stage_terminal` keeps the token count its last request was projected with (`CompressionRuntime`, never persisted). Each later projection books it into the nudge with `NudgeState::absorb_aborted_turn_growth` once the count has moved past it (billion-context's #5415): the growth reference moves on, and from the second interrupt the skipped growth is paid as debt. A restart forgets it.

**Compaction under it.** Under `Hierarchical` the automatic check (`maybe_auto_compact`) ignores the threshold: it compacts on the turn's budget pressure, as before, or when the projection reports the terminal escape (the crate's count of requests over the emergency threshold with nothing left to fold or clip reaching three), and only with `autoCompactionEnabled`. A standard compaction's source is the projection over the active blocks (`project_compaction_source`: covered items out, summaries as text, no tags). When it installs, `after_standard_compaction` restarts the terminal count under `hierarchical`, and the blocks, whose items have left the window, deactivate at the next projection; a session that switched away drops its state.

**Panics.** Every call into the crate runs under `catch_panic`. A panic in a request's projection sends that request with the full history and sets the turn's `compression_fallback`: the turn's later steps run as `standard` (no prompt, tools or nudge, the threshold back), the step records `ContextCompressionFailed` with the panic's message (bounded at 4 KiB) after its `ReasoningUpdated` and logs a warning, and the next turn tries again. A panic while projecting a compaction's source makes the compaction read the full history. A panic in a tool is that call's error, `[Compression FAILED: … (internal error)]`, and leaves the state as it was.

**Settings.** The facade reads `[compaction.hierarchical]` per turn into the turn's extensions as `AgentHierarchicalCompression`, and the kernel maps it onto the crate's `Config` with the session's context window as the model's limit: `maxContextLimitPercent` sets the pressure band, `emergencyThresholdPercent` the emergency nudge and the truncation threshold (never below the band), `nudgeGrowthTokens` the growth step, and the others map by name. A turn without it takes the defaults; with no known context window nothing presses. `absorb`, `contentStore`, `crush` and `rules` turn on the crate's sections of those names.

**Message timestamps.** `messageTimestamps` reaches the kernel as `AgentHierarchicalCompression.message_timestamps`, with `utc_offset_seconds`, the local offset the facade reads (`chrono::Local`) when it admits the turn. History items record no time, so a message's time is a first-sight stamp, as in billion-context's memory-only lanes: a projection passes the crate the clock (`TurnInput.clock`: now, in the turn's offset), and `process_turn` stamps, before minting refs, each message id the ref map does not know yet (`State.ts_stamps`, keyed by message id); an id it knows without a stamp was shown before the setting was on and never gets one. A stamp is a `LocalTime` (milliseconds and the offset it was read in) and is never changed, so a tag renders the same `time="yyyy-mm-dd hh:mm:ss"` (`format_tag_time`, after `type`) on every request, after a restart and after the offset changes (daylight saving, travel); the render reads only the stamps. Only the step's own request keeps its projection's state, so the stamps sent are the ones kept. A message is first shown by the next request after it is committed: a turn's final answer takes the next turn's time. The instructions' tag section is the time-aware variant (two cached prompts). A fold's receipt adds `[start – end]` to each block's fingerprint line, the earliest and latest stamps of the messages it covers and those its range's ends name, or nothing when one has no stamp (billion-context's `blockCoverageTimes`). The stamps travel in the chain as a delta map of their own (new stamps only; one changed or gone takes a snapshot), a fork or a child keeps those of the messages it holds, and a state rebuilt from the `compress` calls has none, so the messages it numbers show no time again. With the setting off no clock is passed: nothing is stamped or shown and the requests are byte-identical to those without timestamps; stamps already kept show again when it is turned back on. The cache report's times of day use the same offset. Not ported: the lean prompt pack's TIMELINE ("Occurred:") rule, since the Agent sends the default pack, which has none.

**What a host sees.** A `compress` call that folds records `ContextCompressed` (its call id, the blocks it made, the tokens they replaced, the highest tier of the blocks it made or refolded) in the commit of its output, right after that commit's `HistoryCommitted` (`CompressionToolRun.fold`, carried by `CompressionChange` into `commit_history_with`); a call that folds nothing records none. The snapshot's `compression` (`AgentCompressionSummary`: the active blocks and `Stats.tokens_compressed`) is read from the state with the snapshot, and left out when there is no state. `AgentService::compression_status` (the facade's, behind `/compression`) copies the window, the state, the content store and the usage under the record's lock; with the lock released it decides the nudge again on a copy of the state (`process_turn_with`, no tags), as billion-context's `/acp` does, so a fold since the last request shows, and builds `build_status_panel`'s panel and the cache report in full. It is tuned with the `[compaction.hierarchical]` a turn would get, commits nothing, and a panic in it is `AgentError::Internal`. A session that neither runs `hierarchical` nor kept a state has none. `compress_call_ranges` reads a `compress` call's arguments with the tool's lenient parser, for the terminal interface's folded cell.

## Installed plugins' content [#package-content]

- **One load, then the sources follow.** A load reads `install.json`, `linked.json` and the cache once (a linked package from the directory its `linked.json` entry names; an `install.json` entry of source `linked`, which the first build of links wrote, is read as one, and the store's next write moves it to `linked.json`; an id in the cache is the cached package even when `linked.json` also names it), sorts the packages by key and reads each enabled one's manifest and components; nothing is cached between loads. It makes each enabled package's data directory, which its hooks and servers are told of. The Skill roots are swapped on the catalog (a changed set drops the cached scan, as a workspace change does), the hook sources on the engine (the next turn's discovery reads them), and the summary for the `plugins` row is replaced; then the MCP servers are reconciled and the code packages, with the linked packages that are on, handed to the guest host ([Plugin guests](#plugin-guests)).
- **Reloads are serialized.** A reload holds its gate from reading the settings to its last change, so of two reloads the later one's set stays. The settings writes start one in the background and do not wait.
- **The MCP side changes only what changed.** Under the MCP configuration gate, a plugin server that is gone, changed or now off is disconnected, unless the user's server of its name has taken the connection over; one that is new or changed connects, at most four at once, and a failed connect is its status. An unchanged server keeps its connection. With no plugin server before or after, the gate is not taken.
- **A hook's package is hashed when it matters.** The runtime discovery at each turn does not hash packages: a plugin's hook trusted by its definition hash is runnable, and the run hashes its package (once per package per event) and refuses the hook when the hash is not the one trusted. A listing hashes each package once, so `/hooks` shows `modified` for a changed package; trusting records both hashes from that listing. A package that cannot be hashed lists its hooks as untrusted and cannot be trusted.
- **What a package's components contribute is its issue when it cannot be used**: a manifest field, a Skill root that does not read (a Skill catalog issue as for any root), an MCP file or server, a hook file (a hooks diagnostic).

## Plugin guests [#plugin-guests]

The guest host ([Code plugins](/docs/architecture#code-plugins)) in detail.

- **States from the fiber.** The supervisor (`plugin-guest-supervisor`, beside the loader in `plugin-guests`) listens to the root's `StatusChanged`, `GuestStarted` and `GuestExited`, and maps a guest fiber to its package through the loader's entry, ignoring a fiber its entry has since replaced. `Loading` is `starting`, `Active` `running`, `Disposed` `stopped`; `Failed` is a failed start when the fiber did not become active since its last load, else a fault. `GuestStarted` keeps the process's handle (its id, its stderr tail) and `GuestExited` its exit report; a start that failed takes the report from the handle, since the bridge watches the exit only once `apply` returned. A process that just ended leaves its guest `running`, without a process id, until its fiber fails.
- **The restart loop.** A fault asks the guest's `RestartWindow` for the next delay. With one, the guest is `restarting` and a job goes to the supervisor's worker, which after the delay checks that nothing was decided about the guest since (each decision, a new package, a retry or a stop, changes its epoch) and that its entry's fiber is still the failed one; then it counts the restart and updates the fiber with its own configuration without saving it (`update_with(config, true)`: Kardia's `restart` keeps a failed fiber failed, and a saved update would be written back to the tree). Without one, the guest stays `failed`. `retry_package` empties the window and updates the failed fiber the same way. A package turned off and on again starts with an empty window.
- **The process.** The spawner runs at each start, on the blocking pool (the bridge instantiates a guest off the async workers): it hashes the package and compares the hash with the recorded one (not a linked package's, which has none), makes the data directory, resolves the command (a `./` path must resolve inside the package; an `interpreter` is an absolute path or a name on the Agent's `PATH`, then readable to the guest too), wraps the command line in the plugin profile, reserves an owner and starts a reserved pipe executor whose stdin, stdout and stderr it hands to the bridge (`start_reserved_pipe_executor`; the session keeps no reader or writer on them). The bridge's `GuestChild` is an adapter over the receipt: `kill` is `terminate_reserved_executor` (SIGTERM to the process group, then SIGKILL; the job object on Windows), `try_wait` and `wait` read the session's end (`wait_reserved_executor`, with the real `ExitStatus`, a signal too), and once the end is observed the executor is disposed, which releases the owner. A child the bridge drops before its end was confirmed is disposed off the dropping thread; one the owner group's sweep already took is gone.
- **Bounds.** The handshake and every export (`apply`, a call, `dispose`) are bounded by `plugin_call_deadline` (10 s) unless the caller sets its own deadline; past it the process is killed. A line from the guest is at most 4 MiB. The supervisor's worker stops with its plugin: the plugin's disposer drops the restart channel before the worker's runs, so the worker ends at once.
- **WebAssembly guests.** The resolver keeps, per entry name, a cell its first resolution fills on the blocking pool: the content check, the module (a `./` path resolved inside the package) read and compiled into a kardia-hmr `WasmGuest` on the `WasmSource` of the package's limits (the time always set, so the engine's epoch interruption is on; the memory; the fuel when given). Guests with the same limits share that source, so one engine and one `wasm-epoch` thread; the resolver keeps a source while a package needs its limits, and a running guest keeps its engine alive until it stops. The plugin is `WasmGuest::plugin` with the manifest's metadata (its name, providing `plugin`), renamed `guest:<id>`: making it instantiates nothing, and each instance's own metadata must match when it starts, or the start fails; a module's `deps` are never read. A module that cannot be read or compiled resolves to a plugin whose every start fails with the reason, so its guest is `failed` rather than left `starting`. Each fiber instantiates the module in a store of its own, so a restart starts from fresh memory; its calls run on the blocking pool, one at a time, each given its fuel and an epoch deadline (its own deadline or the limit, whichever comes first, in 10 ms ticks). Before it makes an engine, the guest host asks kardia-hmr for trap handling by signals on macOS (`set_macos_mach_ports(false)`, which changes nothing elsewhere; see [the panic-hook bridge](/docs/embedding#the-panic-hook-bridge)). A module has no handle, process id, stderr or exit report.
- **A guest instance that failed.** kardia-hmr hands an instance's failure over as a typed `GuestFailure` (`Trap`, `OutOfFuel`, `Timeout`, `OutOfMemory`, `Exited`, `Killed`, `Panicked`), which `GuestHost::call` finds in the call's error (`GuestFailure::of`) and answers by failing the fiber, which the restart loop answers in turn: on any kind but a process guest's `Exited`, `Killed` and `Timeout`, whose process ended or was killed, so kardia-hmr's exit watcher fails the fiber with the exit report. A guest's own error (a status other than 0 with its message) is not a `GuestFailure`: it is only the call's. Either way the call's error text is kardia-hmr's message, unchanged.
- **The plugin profile on the helper's command line.** `--restrict-reads` and one `--readable-root` per root follow the writable roots. Landlock then grants reads under the platform's directories (`/bin`, `/sbin`, `/usr`, `/etc`, `/lib`, `/lib64`, `/nix/store`, `/run/current-system/sw`, after Codex), the readable devices (`/dev/null`, `/dev/zero`, `/dev/random`, `/dev/urandom`), the readable roots and the writable ones, instead of under `/`. Seatbelt names each root by a parameter in `(allow file-read* (subpath …))`, adds Codex's restricted-read platform defaults and leaves out the preferences policy, which reaches data outside the read roots.
- **Logging.** A guest's plugin is named `guest:<id>`, so everything kardia logs for it (its stderr lines, its `log` calls at whatever level, its fiber's failure, which can quote it) carries the prefix, and the host's `LogExporter` forwards such a message only at `trace`, bounded as any content (`bounded_trace_content`). The guest host logs its own decisions (runs, did not start, failed and restarts, exited or was killed with which status) at `info` and `warn`, without the guest's words, which follow at `trace`.
- **Contributions.** The supervisor registers a guest's proxies in its `StatusChanged(Active)` listener, before it marks the guest `running`, through the guest fiber's own context: the `plugin-guests` plugin declares the seven registries and `PluginFaults`, so the guest's checked access finds them and each registration is that fiber's effect (owner `guest:<id>`). What was left out is kept on the guest's record and shown with the kernel's issues for `guest:<id>`. A proxy answers at once while its guest does not run (a tool source fails and, being `Optional`, is omitted; a hook does not apply). A call observes its caller's cancellation by checking it every 50 ms, which drops (and so cancels) the guest call. The prompt contributor asks every running guest with a prompt at once (`join_all`), each within 80% of the call deadline, and sorts the fragments by kind. An observer's worker is a scoped worker of the guest's fiber reading a channel of 64 events.
- **Host services and provisions.** Each package has two loader entries: `<id>#host`, whose plugin (`guest-host:<id>`) provides `workspace` (a view serialized at each read, so the roots are current) and `settings` (its configuration), and the guest's, which isolates `plugin` privately and the two services under the label `guest:<id>`, and `inject`s them, so kardia-loader holds its start in an outer fiber until they run. A guest provides nothing else: kardia-hmr refuses a key its metadata does not declare, so nothing a guest provides reaches the shared realm.
- **Linked packages.** The facade hands the guest host the linked packages that are on, code or not (`GuestHost::follow_linked`), at construction and with every reload; the guest host watches them from the supervisor's start to its disposer, and a changed set (by id and path key) replaces the watch. The watch is a thread (`plugin-linked-watcher`) of the instance's contained worker group, which arms a recursive OS watch (`notify`) on each directory that is one, so a change made before it armed is not seen; a directory missing, removed (its `Remove` event) or gone is tried again at every check and armed once it is a directory again, which counts as its change, so an export that replaces the directory is followed; it checks its token every 100 ms, so the group's shutdown or the guest host's stop ends it. An event is attributed by path key: a read (`Access`) or a path in a directory's `.git` is no change, and an event that names no path under a directory (an overflow, a rescan) changes them all. Once the changes have stopped for 500 ms, each directory that changed counts one more revision and `GuestHost::linked_changes` moves. The `plugins` row's scoped worker (`plugins-linked`) waits on that counter and spawns the facade's reload in the background (`PackageReload`, which `reload_packages` also runs: the packages read, then the role catalog rebuilt), so changes made while one runs are taken by one more. The reload's `apply` finds the guest's revision changed: a new epoch and a new entry name (`linked <revision>` in what is digested), which kardia-loader answers by disposing the old fiber and starting the new plugin, a module compiled anew. An installed package's name does not carry the revision, so it is unchanged.
- **What reaches the settings.** The group update writes nothing back, and a fault, a restart or a failed start changes no configuration; only the loader's own write-back, a plugin that disposed itself, reaches `[plugins]`, as `enabled = false`. A guest's environment still carries the executor's final overlay (the proxy's variables), which a host sets for every child.

## Other details [#other-details]

### The instance, the slot and the start [#instance-and-start]

- **The runtime.** Each Agent instance's `ContainedRuntime` has between 2 and 4 worker threads (the machine's parallelism, clamped) and up to 128 blocking threads, all named `agent-rt` (`AGENT_RUNTIME_THREAD_NAME`) and marked contained. The slot keeps it (`AgentSlot::take_runtime` hands it to the stop), and the instance's stop ends it last with `ContainedRuntime::shutdown`, the hard stop that drops whatever the instance still runs (a plugin `apply` that never returned, a disposal waiting on it) without touching the host. The Agent's `BlockingOperationPort` is `ContainedRuntime::blocking_port`.
- **Per-instance sinks.** Each instance reports to the slot through sinks tied to its generation: kernel faults through `AgentSlot::fault_sink(generation)`, history refusals through `AgentSlot::refusal_sink` (`AgentInstanceInputs::history_refusals`). A stale instance's report is ignored, and a fault during a start fails that start. A quarantined session, from recovery at start or refused since, is added to the status once and announced. `generation` in the status counts the starts claimed in this run; `degraded` is derived from the plugin status.
- **Kernel faults** come from a turn's task (`execute_admitted_turn` catches a panic around the loop and the managed task's `JoinError` is checked; the sink is injected into the kernel with `AgentService::without_default_model_reporting_faults`), a host's command guard, a member of the Agent's worker groups (`BackgroundWorkerGroup::contained_with_panic_handler`: history writers, the memory supervisor, MCP stdio readers; reported as `worker <name>`), and the host's and service's own tasks (`spawn_kernel_task`: the plugin-fault follower, a remount, the settings follow-up, the MCP start). The slot marks the Agent `Faulted`, stops it on its supervisor thread (`agent-supervisor`) and starts the next instance after 2 s, 10 s and 30 s for the first three faults in 10 minutes (`AUTOMATIC_RESTART_DELAYS`, `AUTOMATIC_RESTART_WINDOW`); after that it stays `Faulted` with no `nextRestartAt` until the user restarts it, which empties the window.
- **Transitions.** `AgentSlot::restart(AgentStartOptions { safe_mode, rebuild_history_index })` is refused while the Agent starts, while the host shuts down and while the host has turned it off (`Disabled` with `setting`), but starts an Agent a crash loop stopped; `enable` and `turn_off` (`disable`, then a bounded wait) are the host's on and off. Each schedules the supervisor thread. A restart stops the instance with the shutdown sequence and budget (`stop_instance`), sweeps its process owner group, stops its runtime, resets the crash-loop marker (for the user's restart) and starts again. Safe mode keeps every row outside the core unmounted for that instance (`AgentHostOptions::safe_mode`; `disabledPlugins` is unchanged). `disable` cancels a start under way and leaves `Disabled` with `setting`. The Skill watcher restarts on each new instance's roots.
- **The call that lost its Agent.** `AgentSlot::interrupted(generation, command)` gives the error for a call whose Agent no longer runs: "the Agent was turned off; `<command>` did not finish", "the Agent stopped after an internal error; `<command>` did not finish" (faulted with no restart scheduled), else "the Agent restarted; `<command>` did not finish".
- **The start.** `DesktopAgent::start` adds the Agent's own `ExecutorManager` (over the child-process environment `DesktopAgent` was built with) and Skill watcher, hands them to the slot for every later instance and starts the first. The start (`startup`) claims a generation, builds the runtime and the process owner group (`ProcessOwnerGroup::recorded_in`), takes the data directory's lock once per run, records a crash-loop attempt, reaps the process groups an earlier run left (`ProcessGroupRecord::reap_leftovers`), constructs the service on the blocking pool (the `AgentConstructor` gets the runtime and the instance's `AgentInstanceInputs`, its Executor already scoped to the owner group), runs `recover` there (which also settles the execution activities the previous process left running), starts the plugins, schedules the MCP connects and installs the service as `Running`; then the Skill watcher starts on the instance's roots. A failure at any stage is logged at error, leaves the slot `Failed` and clears the attempt.
- **What fails construction:** the settings (an I/O error), the workspace roots port and the workspace policy, the network port's proxy, the MCP settings file (an I/O error only; it repairs itself) and an invalid MCP proxy policy, the history store's layout or index, and the memory store. A construction that fails part-way stops the worker groups it already started. What does not fail it: a Skill catalog that does not open (every Skill command returns the reason and the `skills` plugin fails with it), a required MCP server that does not connect (its MCP status, `Failed`, logged at error; every server is tried before the start reports), a `web` row whose configuration does not derive (that row's error).
- **Cancelling a start.** `AgentSlot::close` (shutdown) and `disable` cancel a start's token. Construction and recovery block and cannot be cancelled, so the start checks the token between stages; the plugins' start is cancelled where it stands. A built Agent that will not run (its recovery or start failed, or the start was cancelled or the slot closed meanwhile) is retired first: turns, MCP (background connects cancelled, their processes disposed), plugins and workers stop in shutdown order within `RETIRE_BOUND` (5 s), shorter than shutdown's wait for a start (`START_WAIT`, 8 s), so its runtime stops only after that teardown. A start still blocked in construction or recovery then has started no plugin, connection or process, so the runtime's hard stop leaves only its detached blocking thread.

### The plugin host [#plugin-host-details]

- **Mounting.** `AgentHost` creates nothing itself: composition creates the `AgentKernelRegistries`, constructs the kernel with them (so the `titles` plugin's `AgentTitleApplication` can hold the kernel) and hands them to the host. A row that does not settle within `PLUGIN_START_TIMEOUT` fails with "did not start within 20 s", its fiber is disposed in the background and the next row mounts. Every model operation (a turn, a plan implementation, a compaction) starts the host if nothing did; the kernel plans from registry snapshots and never waits for it. A panic in a host task (the fault follower, a remount) is a kernel fault at `plugin host`.
- **Every registry is built with the kernel's one `PluginFaults`** (`AgentKernelRegistries::faults`, also published by the kernel plugin), and each entry holds it. The registries that combine entries themselves guard each one: `ObserverRegistry::notify`, `ApprovalRuleSnapshot::verdict`, `PromptContributorSnapshot::fragments`. `ExtensionPoint` converts into the fault's `FaultPoint`. At most 64 continuation hooks can be registered (`MAXIMUM_CONTINUATION_HOOKS`).
- **Applying settings.** After the host saves its settings, `AgentSlot::apply_saved_settings` schedules `apply_settings_to_plugins` on the running Agent's runtime; the Agent's own settings writes (`write_settings`, `set_plugin_enabled`) call it after theirs. Neither waits. The host reads the latest settings under its plugin lock, so the later of two quick saves wins; disposes a row `disabledPlugins` now names; mounts one it no longer names, with its current configuration, once its previous fiber has stopped; and calls `Fiber::update` only where a settings row's configuration changed. It does not wait for the transitions, and logs each outcome. A row not mounted yet takes the new configuration and enablement for its mount.
- **The settings API.** `settings_view` returns an `AgentSettingsView`: the settings, their defaults and the web tools' form fields. `write_settings` takes an `AgentSettingsPatch` (the permission mode, memory, the web search provider, `searxngBaseUrl`, where `null` clears it and a missing key leaves it, and `webFetchUseJinaReader`), an IPC request that rejects unknown fields and has no `disabledPlugins`, which only `set_plugin_enabled` writes. It applies the patch as a whole or not at all, brings the plugins up to date and signals a memory scan. The Provider registry changes only through `AgentConfig::mutate_providers` with the revision the caller expects.
- **The MCP proxy follow-up** runs as a task of its own: it reads the latest settings under the MCP configuration gate and, when the proxy policy changed, reconnects the enabled Streamable HTTP servers concurrently, so a reconnect that hangs holds neither the command nor a later change, which applies after it.
- **Bounds.** A re-enabled row waits for its previous fiber only `PLUGIN_STOP_BOUND` (10 s), then fails with "its previous instance has not stopped yet" until a retry after the teardown ended.
- **Enablement and status.** The core rows ignore `disabledPlugins`; a name no row carries is kept and ignored. `set_plugin_enabled` refuses an unknown or core name and writes through `AgentConfig::set_plugin_disabled` on the blocking boundary, serialized by the file's lock. After start-up the host logs every enabled plugin that is not `Active`. A status's `contributions` are read from one snapshot of each kernel registry by entry owner: tool names, tool source ids, context contributors with their kinds, turn admission and prompt contributor ids, approval rule ids and observer ids. `restart` is a pending automatic restart, or the ones the window counts.
- **The plugin event sink.** The host tells its `AgentPluginEventSink` when start-up ends, when a row is enabled, disabled, retried or reconfigured, when such a transition settles, when a disabled row's teardown ends (its registrations are gone only then), and when a plugin's issues appear, change or clear (`PluginFaults::set_issues_listener`, so a contributor failing the same way every turn announces nothing more).

### Sessions and turns [#turn-details]

- **Configuration.** `normalize_session_config` runs at creation, every update and recovery: remote compaction on a protocol other than Responses (`ModelApiProtocol::supports_remote_compaction`), and a reasoning effort or remote compaction on an unconfigured session, are dropped with a warning (every protocol carries an effort); a zero context window becomes unknown; the auto-compact threshold and the stream retry policy are clamped. Recovery normalizes an older session again instead of quarantining it; the identity (Provider id, model) and the integrity checks stay strict. A session's compaction strategy, and the compaction capability its transport carries, are reconciled with the settings at each activation while the session is idle, and committed without a new revision: no snapshot shows them, so a caller's revision read before the operation stays current. A config written before the strategy existed, or with one this build does not know, reads as `standard`.
- **Recovery** (`service/recovery/`: validation, the persisted record, the restart repairs in `repair.rs`). Validation rejects a session that persisted a counter the kernel later increments (revision, generation, message, history item, turn, compaction and activity ids, steering sequence, model session generation, memory contribution and history item revisions) above `u64::MAX / 2` (`validation/counters.rs`), so the kernel's `checked_add(1)` expectations are invariants. `load_recovery_candidates` loads each candidate on its own and returns, as failures, the sessions the history store refused at open whatever their recovery state; `recover_sessions` returns an `AgentRecoveryReport` (the count restored and the `AgentQuarantinedSession`s with their reasons) after logging each skipped session at error. Opening a quarantined session fails with its unresolved recovery obligation, or the store's reason, until a later start recovers it. The memory source projection leaves out a session whose runtime does not decode. Only a failure to read the history index fails recovery.
- **Ids.** Turn and compaction ids come from atomic counters.
- **Calls the step cannot run** (`service/tool_execution/rejection.rs`, `CallDisposition`): history records a call to a name the step does not offer under the `unavailable` provider and tool id. Arguments over `maximum_tool_argument_bytes` (64 KiB) keep `{}` in place of what the model sent ([Model tool arguments](/docs/plugin-rules#model-tool-arguments)).
- **`tool_search` selections.** A `ConversationItem::ToolSelection` (`call_id`, the selected `AgentToolSelection`s) is not model input: on a provider without native search the selected tools become `Direct` in the plan, and on one with it the replayed search output shows them. A selection whose tool is gone, or whose generation changed, matches nothing; history from before the item existed simply has none.
- **Turn limits** read, for the user: "agent turn stopped at its time limit of 30 minutes; send a message to continue" (`TurnDeadlineExceeded`), and "the conversation is still too long for the model's context window after it was compacted; start a new conversation or attach less context" (`ContextWindowExceededAfterCompaction`).
- **World state.** A world state holds at most 12 sections (`MAXIMUM_AGENT_WORLD_STATE_SECTIONS`: one per known kind, 5, and 7 plugin kinds); a persisted update may name twice that, a replace and a remove per kind. The instructions are not world state: the session's configured instructions (by default the base instructions with the host's `AgentHostInstructions` joined in), plus the kernel's `plan.md` in Plan mode.
- **Context items the kernel frames.** The kernel frames the context items that are history rather than sections: a compacted window's initial context, the task checklist and a selected Skill's instructions (`SkillContext`). The model API frames only its own items: continuation context, compaction summaries and the remote compaction item.
- **Host context.** Recovery holds persisted steering to `MAXIMUM_HOST_CONTEXT_BYTES` (4 KiB). Steering recorded before the host context had a kind (as `applicationContext`) reads back without one.
- **Compaction.** A standalone compaction, outside any sampling step, builds the tool plan and prompt its next request would have, with nothing prepared, so its request has no memory context (`PromptRequest::prepared` is `None`).
- **Background compaction** (`service/compaction/background/`). The slot (`SessionRuntime.background_compaction`) holds the last attempt's inputs (window, the number of items before the current turn, model, implementation), which are not launched again, so a failed run is retried only once another turn has completed, and its run, shared by `Arc`: a rolled-back commit restores the record's runtime, but the outcome (running, ready with its plan and sample, being installed, installed with its outcome, or spent) lives in the run, where the task writes it without the record's lock, then advances `terminal`. A checkpoint that takes the result marks it being installed and, when it stops, installed or spent, advancing `terminal`; a waiting `/compact` that finds it installed by a turn admitted meanwhile returns that outcome instead of compacting with the revision it read. The task is registered in the service's `managed_background` set before the record's lock is released, so `stop_operations` either sees the slot to cancel or the launch sees that the service stopped accepting; shutdown waits for the set, archiving and deletion for the session's entries (5 s). The plan is built as a pre-turn compaction's is, from the step's model, tool plan and prompt, reserves nothing in `runtime.compaction` and checks its own cancellation token. When it ends with a sample its usage is committed to the session's (not a revision, and its `context_consumed_tokens` kept), so the install adds none.
- **The rebasing install** (`InstallMode::Rebase`) replaces the compare-and-swap: no compaction runs, the window is the snapshot's, and the history begins with the snapshot's item ids (the whole history at launch). The installed history is the world state where the uncompacted part begins (`full_world_state_checkpoint` of the history before the boundary: the updates after it, removals included, still apply in order; the plan keeps that boundary, `source_boundary`, apart from the items the compaction read, which a session with active compression blocks reads projected and so fewer), the replacement, then the history from the current turn's `TurnStart` at launch onward with its original ids, as a pre-turn install keeps the current turn (after the same checkpoint: the world state where that turn begins). The checkpoint's cutoff stays the display summary's id, so every replacement id is at or below it and resume and forks replay it as any compaction; item ids are therefore not in history order after it. The install records a `Completed` snapshot of phase `background` (trigger `automatic`, reason `context_limit`, the launching turn's generation) and its event, charges no turn's budget, and estimates the next request with the adopting step's tool plan and prompt: still over the threshold is not an error there, the caller compacts in line. A result that does not reduce the context is refused like any other.
- **The context window.** Selecting a model again resolves its window again and moves the recorded usage to the new window.
- **Continuation hooks.** A hook cancelled because its plugin stopped completes as `IgnoredFailure`.
- **Observers.** `TurnAdmitted` carries the turn's origin (`User` or `Programmatic`) and the provisional title its admission committed; `MemoryContributionChanged` its cause (`Initial`, `User` or `Polluted`). `start_turn` and plan implementations are `Programmatic`.
- **Child sessions** (`service/session/child.rs`). A child takes its parent's model, configuration, budget policy and workspace, its scope narrowed by the request's and its role instructions the request's when the request names a role (checked against the session bounds, since they do not come from the parent); one from the parent's head also takes its title, so the `titles` plugin does not title it, and its memory contribution starts at the default. A durable child is persisted as a fork at the head (`create_fork_at_head`) or before the running turn (`create_fork`), an empty one as a new session. History starts with a turn, so context can only follow inherited history: a child that would start empty with context is refused (`InvalidInput`), as is context that is not continuation context. An ephemeral child of an ephemeral session reads its images from the nearest durable ancestor. A durable child initializes its memory contribution as a fork does; an ephemeral one never does.
- **Ephemeral sessions.** `close_ephemeral_session` cancels the session's turn or compaction, aborts its pending approvals, waits up to 5 s for it to settle, then removes the cell, its event sink and its in-memory history; a turn that has not settled by then fails on the missing session. Archiving or restoring an ephemeral session is refused (`InvalidInput`). The in-memory store pages a transcript as the durable one does (newest first when descending, index cursors) and filters a turn's page by its turn id, but keeps no revisions: a page is always the session's latest.
- **The side conversation's items** are two `ContinuationContext` items under the provider `agent`, the hooks `side-conversation` (the policy, in `<side_conversation>` markers) and `side-conversation-boundary` (the boundary), generation 1 (`crates/agent/src/side_conversation.rs`, ported from Codex's `context-fragments/src/side_conversation.rs`). The facade finds a parent's side conversation among the kernel's live ephemeral children (`ephemeral_children`) under a gate, so opening it twice at once still yields one.

### Thinking [#thinking-details]

Beside [Thinking](/docs/architecture#thinking):

- The kernel's `AgentReasoningId` is drawn from the session's message counter (`service/sampling/stream/reasoning.rs`).
- A new summary part arrives with the blank line that separates it. The text kept is the completed item's summary when it has one, else its raw text, each from the completed item when it carries any, else as streamed; text the completed item adds to what was shown is shown before the completion.
- A response of only reasoning still commits nothing.
- An Anthropic thinking block to send back rides on its `ConversationItem::Reasoning` as `replay` (`#[serde(skip)]`): in memory only, never persisted, never in a snapshot, never counted in the history's bytes. A step's request places the current turn's blocks (after its last `TurnStart`) as `ModelInputItem::Reasoning`, which only Anthropic Messages with a thinking budget sends; Responses and Chat Completions leave them out. A compaction's request carries none.
- The timing snapshot (`ReasoningUpdated`, `AgentReasoningSnapshot`) is kept beside the items for status and recovery.

### Titles [#titles]

- The facade's submission marks a turn `User` and, for a session without a title, passes the submitted text collapsed to one line and cut to 36 characters as the kernel's `AgentTurnInput::provisional_title`. The admission commits it with the turn if the session still has no title (`session_title_updated` follows `turn_started` and `history_committed`). Neither the origin nor the provisional title is persisted with the turn.
- The `titles` plugin's observer queues (bounded, `try_send`) every `TurnAdmitted` of a `User` turn that committed a provisional title. Its worker (`spawn_scoped_worker`, one title at a time, stopped with the plugin) reads the turn's user message (`AgentService::turn_user_message`) and the session's Provider from the current settings through `AgentTitleApplication` on the `BlockingService`, asks a private model session for a title (the prompt cut to 960 bytes, 30 s), and commits it with `replace_automatic_session_title_if_expected`, a compare-and-swap on the provisional title, so a rename meanwhile wins. With the plugin off the provisional title stays.
- The request is the shared structured request (`plugins/structured_request.rs`): a JSON schema output where the provider has one (`ModelStructuredOutputCapability::NativeJsonSchema`), otherwise a forced call of a tool whose arguments are the object; bounded in time and cancelled with the plugin. A recap and a title suggestion use it too.
- A title suggestion (`suggest_session_title`) reads the session's latest 8 user and assistant messages through `AgentConversationReader` (newest first, from the transcript), each cut to an equal share of the 960 bytes, and commits nothing.
- A recap (`recap_session`, the `recap` plugin) reads the latest 8 answered exchanges the same way (steering joins its request, a newer unanswered request is kept, no tool output or reasoning), within a prompt of 8192 estimated tokens (oldest exchanges omitted first, then each text excerpted keeping both ends), and returns `AgentRecap { summary, next_action }` (60 s). The answer is read tolerantly: unknown fields ignored, a missing `next_action` is none, a summary over 700 or a next action over 200 characters is cut.

### Storage layout [#storage-layout]

Beside [Data directory](/docs/data-directory#contents):

- `history/active/YYYY/MM/DD/session-<YYYY-MM-DD>T<HH-MM-SS>-<id>.jsonl` (local time of its creation), `history/archived/`, `history/attachments/<id>/blobs/<sha256>.blob`, `history/attachments/<id>/exports/` (a session's export files, `decompress` with `toFile`), `history/trash/` and `history/index.sqlite3`. Forks share the parent's prefix through the lineage table. The index is schema 6: 5 added `agent_sessions.parent_session_id` (indexed) for the parent filter of the session list, 6 the hierarchical compression chain's `agent_compression_links`; an index of another version, an older one included, is discarded at open and rebuilt from the rollouts, once.
- `memory/memory.sqlite3`, `memory/ad-hoc-notes.jsonl` (the model's notes), `memory/artifacts/` (the published artifacts, `MEMORY.md` and `memory_summary.md` among them), `memory/staging/` and `memory/reset-trash/`.

### The memory store [#memory-store]

Beside [Memory](/docs/memory#how):

- **A pass** pages through the idle history sessions newest first (`AgentMemoryHistoryQuery::older_than`, 100 a page, at most 1000 a pass) and claims up to `maxSessionsPerPass` of them. Whether a session may be extracted (contributing, a revision not yet extracted, no backoff, no lease) is the memory store's decision, so newer sessions already extracted never keep an older one waiting.
- **Phase one's evidence budget** is 70% of the extraction model's context window as a session would get it (`AgentService::model_context_window`: configured, else the [model metadata](/docs/architecture#model-metadata)'s, else 160K), at most 120K tokens.
- **Backoff**, 30 s doubling to a day: phase one per source (`memory_sources.retry_count`, `next_retry_at`), phase two globally (`memory_meta.global_retry_count`, `global_next_retry_at`); a success or a reset clears it. A citation usage change that moves the ranked cutoff of a running consolidation fails its publish as stale, which the backoff retries.
- **Publish** stages and validates the artifacts (`MEMORY.md` at most 128 KiB, `memory_summary.md` at most 16 KiB and starting with its `v1` line), commits the database (the new generation is then the published one) and only then swaps them in. A crash or a failed rename after the commit leaves them in `staging/generation-<n>`, which the next open or the next consolidation claim installs before anything reads the artifacts as a baseline.
- **Opening** migrates a version-4 database in place (it lacked the phase-two backoff; the discard path would also remove the published artifacts). It discards the database, with the derived artifacts, only when it is not one, is damaged (`SQLITE_CORRUPT`, `SQLITE_NOTADB`) or has another schema; a busy, locked or I/O failure fails the open, so the Agent's construction fails with the reason and a restart retries. Every connection waits up to 5 s for a lock (`busy_timeout`), as the history index's do. The schema is version 5. `memory reset` is the one discard a user triggers.
- **Tolerance.** The notes log skips a line it cannot read (logged by line number, never its content); a log over 64 MiB is still `Corrupt`. Phase-one output ignores fields its schema does not name: `hasMemory: false` is the no-op gate and wins over stray fields; otherwise a missing text is empty, an over-long one is cut on a character boundary, NULs are dropped and a bad `rolloutSlug` is rewritten to lowercase kebab-case (`memory` when nothing is left); only output that is not an extraction object fails the job. A contribution mode the store does not know reads as `disabled`.
- **The contribution mode** is one type, `hanafubuki_agent_foundation::agent_settings::AgentMemoryContributionMode` (default `Disabled`), which the kernel persists with the session and the store per source, re-exported by the contracts and the memory crate.
- **Signals.** The `memory` plugin's observer signals a scan when a turn has finished (so a pass never competes with a live turn for the model), queues citation usage after an answer commits, records a committed contribution change (signalling a scan for the user's toggle and whenever a session stops contributing; a new or forked session that starts contributing is picked up by a later scan) and signals a scan when a session is archived or restored. Memory's own operations (start-up, a note, a reset, a revoked session) and a settings change signal scans directly.

### MCP connections, catalogs and output [#mcp-details]

Beside [MCP](/docs/architecture#mcp) and [External producers](/docs/plugin-rules#external-producers-mcp-servers-skills-web-memory):

- **Start.** The Agent's start connects the enabled servers in the background (`service/mcp/startup.rs`), 4 at a time (`MCP_CONNECT_CONCURRENCY`), each bounded by its own connect and initialize timeouts, so the Agent is `Running` before they connect; a hung server ends `failed` with "MCP operation timed out". Every connect observes the MCP lifetime token, which shutdown cancels first, and a cancelled connect disposes the process it spawned.
- **Closing.** `McpManager::shutdown(deadline)` closes every connection, each within its `shutdown_timeout` and all by the deadline; one not confirmed closed stays retired with its cleanup obligation (`cleanup_pending` in its status). A connection whose server side ended is retired and its process disposed with its receipt once no step holds it.
- **Reconnect.** One restart window per server (`McpManager::with_reconnect_policy`); the status carries `reconnect` (the next attempt, or none once used up). A reconnect does not start once a connect, refresh or disconnect from outside got there first, and one that completes after shutdown began disposes its process instead of installing it. A stdio server's reason names how it ended (`the MCP server process exited with code 3`).
- **Panics.** A panic in a tool call's client path makes the call "the tool failed with an internal error", and `McpManager::fail_after_panic` retires the connection and reconnects the server as above (`failed` with "the MCP client panicked during a tool call: `<message>`", diagnostic code `tool_call_panicked`).
- **Identity.** A tool's generation is a digest of the server's configuration, its credential generations and the tool's own catalog entry and policies. A reconnect of an unchanged server keeps it, so a session's `AllowForSession` of the tool survives the reconnect; a change to the server's settings, a credential's `generation` or the tool asks again.
- **Catalog.** Built tool by tool from paged `tools/list` results. A tool skipped (an invalid or oversized schema, a name that cannot be encoded, a later duplicate after sanitizing, `my.tool` becoming `my_tool`) is a `McpCatalogIssue`, recorded as a warning with the tool's name (at most 16 per listing) beside a `tool_discovery_partial` summary. The bound of 2048 tools a server stays strict, counts undecodable entries, and is checked once; `nextCursor: ""` ends paging; a page over 4 MiB or with an oversized cursor is an error. Calls go out by the raw remote name.
- **Lists and reads.** `tools/list`, both resource lists and `resources/read` are requested through `Peer::send_request` (`listing.rs`, `request::read_resource`), so a result rmcp's typed structs reject arrives as `ServerResult::CustomResult` and is decoded entry by entry: an undecodable tool is a `tool_skipped_undecodable` catalog issue; an undecodable resource or template is left out with a `resource_skipped_undecodable` or `resource_template_skipped_undecodable` warning (at most 16 per listing) counted in `resource_discovery_partial`; an undecodable read entry is the text `[unsupported MCP content: resource]`. These requests bypass rmcp's response cache.
- **Output.** A mime type with parameters is stored as its base type and unpadded base64 is accepted, both stored canonical (`canonical_media_type`, `canonical_base64`).

### Execution and files [#execution-files]

- `exec_command` runs through reserved Executor processes; the `execution` plugin's activity monitor attaches as the execution's observer, and disposing a finished execution waits, bounded, until the monitor has reported the final output and status. The activity preview commits only lines that end in a newline and shows the unfinished one provisionally. Results are plain text (`render_plain_text`). The generic argv `start` exists only under `test-support`.
- One caller disposes an execution: `dispose` claims it first, and to every other caller a claimed or disposed execution is gone (`NotFound`), even one whose receipt it fetched before. So a model's wait that a background terminal's stop overtakes ends in `NotFound`, or in the terminal result the stop caused when the wait saw it first, never in a process or cleanup error. A failed disposal drops its claim and keeps the execution, owing cleanup, for a retry.
- `WorkspaceFsService` resolves every request into an immutable plan within the policy's roots, revalidates before I/O and writes atomically; `preview_patch` is pure. The directories `WorkspaceRootsPort::protected_roots` names are the policy's protected roots: every file tool, reads included, refuses a path under them ("direct filesystem tools cannot access application-owned path …", `ProtectedPath`).

### Shutdown [#shutdown-details]

Beside [the stages](/docs/embedding#start-on-and-off-shutdown):

- Each stage ends by its own deadline or by the end of the budget, whichever comes first, and a stage that misses it is reported while the next one runs. Only a `Running` Agent gets the stages after closing the slot.
- The worst cases stay inside the 20 s: a start under way takes at most 8 s plus the runtime's 2 s; a running Agent at most 5 + 4 + 5 + 3 + 1 + 2 = 20 s. The Skill watcher's thread normally ends at once; one held inside a catalog invalidation takes up to its 2 s from what is left.
- The MCP and plugin stages run on the Agent's runtime and are awaited with `ContainedRuntime::block_on_until`, whose wait does not depend on the runtime making progress, so a runtime whose workers are all blocked cannot hold the exit.
- A host's own teardown follows (Hanafubuki stops its presentation dispatcher afterwards, with a 2 s deadline of its own).

### The terminal interface [#tui-details]

Beside [What the port changed](/docs/architecture#what-the-port-of-the-terminal-interface-changed):

- **Thinking.** The bridge maps `ReasoningStarted`, `ReasoningDelta` and `ReasoningCompleted` onto a thinking cell (`history_cell/thinking.rs`) that lives in the viewport beside the running tool calls. On `ReasoningCompleted` the cell goes into the scrollback as shown, with the kernel's `durationMillis`; a turn's end completes a live cell with the time so far, since a turn that fails mid-stream sends no completion. While a message streams, the reasoning updates wait behind it as tool updates do, so the scrollback keeps the model's order. Ctrl-O toggles one flag of the chat widget, for the live cells and every later one. The transcript pager draws each cell's `transcript_lines`, which for thinking always include the text; the resume replay maps `ConversationItem::Reasoning` to a completed cell. The live text is the streamed text: in the rare item that streams both a summary and raw text, the committed item keeps the summary, which a resumed session shows.
- **Sessions.** The interface opens every session through the bridge (`AgentBridge::open`, over `open_session`): a new one, one `resume` names, the latest, or one picked in the resume picker. The latest transcript items are read back into what the chat replays (`agent_bridge/replay.rs`: messages, thinking, plans, tool calls with their output and the `apply_patch` diff read from their committed items), after the header. `/new` and `/resume` switch the bridge's session, and the events of the one left are ignored.
- **Commands** map onto the facade in `agent_bridge/commands.rs`: `/new` `create_session`; `/resume` `list_sessions_page` (searched by title, cursor-paged, read on the loop as the picker scrolls); `/compact` `compact_session_with_sink`; `/model` `list_provider_models` and `set_session_model`; `/mode` `set_session_mode`; `/status` `settings_view`, `provider_registry_snapshot` and `workspace_roots`; `/skills` `list_skills`. `/providers` and `/mcp` have a module each (`agent_bridge/providers.rs`, `agent_bridge/mcp.rs`): `/providers` onto `provider_registry_snapshot`, `test_provider_draft`, `write_provider`, `delete_provider`, `set_provider_enabled`, `write_model_policy` and `list_provider_models`, each write with the revision the screen read and, when it is refused, the registry read again if it moved; `/mcp` onto `mcp_snapshot` (also `/mcp verbose`), `mcp_write_server` then `mcp_set_credential` for its secrets and `mcp_remove_credential` for the ones no server references any more, `mcp_delete_server`, `mcp_connect`, `mcp_refresh`, `mcp_oauth_logout`, and `mcp_oauth_login` on the Agent's runtime, cancelled by dropping the sender its `cancelled` future waits on. The approval and answer calls, which do not wait, run on the loop.
- **Compactions.** The bridge turns each `CompactionUpdated` whose status is `completed` into the `Context compacted` cell, whatever started the compaction, and refreshes the status line. `/compact`'s reply carries only a failure, so a manual compaction shows once, and so does one `/compact` takes from the background. A `CompactionUpdated` that is `failed` with trigger `automatic` becomes a warning cell, kept in `/warnings` (source `compaction`); a manual one's failure is `/compact`'s reply, and a cancelled one shows nothing. While a message streams the cell waits behind it, as tool updates do. `/status` reads `backgroundCompaction` from the snapshot it takes when it runs: the background slot changes without a revision.
- **Overlays.** The transcript pager, the `/diff` pager and the resume picker take over the alternate screen; the cells committed meanwhile wait and are written into the scrollback when the overlay closes, and the terminal's restore leaves the alternate screen when one is open (a fatal panic's restore included).
- **File search** walks the workspace roots with `ignore` (a root nested in another is walked once, as its own) into a `nucleo` matcher on its own threads, re-ranked as the `@token` changes.
- **The binary** composes and starts the Agent as for `exec`, with the interface's status sink (`TuiEvents::status_sink`), hands it the running handle (`hanafubuki_agent_tui::run`) and shuts the Agent down with `AGENT_SHUTDOWN_BUDGET` once the interface has restored the terminal. `exec` ends when the Agent's status changes during the turn (a kernel fault), since the restarted Agent has a new generation.
- **The panic hook.** `logging::logging_blocked`, which the binary passes to `install_panic_hook`, says whether the thread is inside the file logger; the hook then writes to standard error only.

### Known limits [#internal-limits]

- A `tool_catalog_omissions` update first appended when a step begins is not in that step's admission estimate.
- rmcp's untagged `ServerResult` decodes a result that carries `_meta` and an undecodable entry as a `CallToolResult`, whose entries are already gone, so such a listing page still fails whole.

### A host's side [#host-side]

What a host builds around the Agent is the host's to document; Hanafubuki's (its command guard in `src/agent/commands/guard.rs`, its event sinks and window events, its Task Center rows from `AgentSlot::list_run_projections`, its diagnostic bundle's `app/agent-status.json` and `app/agent-plugins.json` from `flush_for_diagnostics` and `plugin_status_for_diagnostics`, its `agent` module row and quit order, the one-time import of its older settings files) is in its [agent-integration.md](https://github.com/licyk/Hanafubuki/blob/main/docs/agent-integration.md). What every host's command layer does the same way: it resolves the slot per call, runs the call on the Agent's runtime, turns a panic in it into the call's error and a kernel fault, and answers a call whose Agent stopped under it with `AgentSlot::interrupted`.
